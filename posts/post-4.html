The tumor immune microenvironment, explained

Science note · Starter article

Tumors are not just cancer cells; they are ecosystems of immune, stromal, and vascular components.

The tumor microenvironment includes cancer cells, immune cells, fibroblasts, blood vessels, extracellular matrix, and signaling molecules. Some components support immune attack, while others can restrict T-cell entry or function. Describing a tumor as immune-inflamed, excluded, or desert-like is a useful simplification, but real tumors can contain several states at once. Understanding these relationships can help researchers design therapies that recruit or activate antitumor immunity.
