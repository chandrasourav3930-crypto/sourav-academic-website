How do vaccines create immune memory?

Science note · Starter article

Vaccines prepare the immune system to respond more effectively to a future encounter with a pathogen or antigen.

Vaccines expose the immune system to an antigen or instructions for making one, in a form intended to generate protective immunity without causing the target disease. Antigen-presenting cells help activate lymphocytes, and some activated B and T cells develop into long-lived memory populations. The strength and duration of protection depend on the vaccine platform, antigen, host, and pathogen.
