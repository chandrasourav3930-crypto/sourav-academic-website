How oncolytic viruses turn tumors into immune targets

Science note · Starter article

A short introduction to how oncolytic viruses selectively infect or kill tumor cells and can also help alert the immune system to cancer.

Oncolytic viruses are designed or selected to preferentially replicate in cancer cells. Their effects can extend beyond direct tumor-cell killing: infection may release tumor antigens and inflammatory signals that help immune cells recognize the tumor. The outcome depends on the virus, tumor biology, pre-existing antiviral immunity, and the surrounding tumor microenvironment. A key challenge is balancing antiviral clearance with enough viral activity to generate a useful antitumor response.
